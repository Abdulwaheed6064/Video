# Offer, promise and pricing

## 1. Why "top of Google Maps for anyone in Toronto" doesn't work as written

Google ranks local results on three things: **relevance** (does the business match the search),
**distance** (how far the business is from the searcher or the place named in the search) and
**prominence** (reviews, links, how well-known it is). You can improve relevance and prominence.
You cannot move the shop.

Consequences you must design around:

- "Pest control Toronto" is not one ranking. A searcher in North York sees a different 3-pack from one in
  the Beaches. Toronto is ~630 km²; a single-location business will realistically own a radius, not the city.
- Small towns are different. "Barber shop Bradford" has a handful of competitors in a small area —
  top 3 across most of Bradford is a realistic 20–60 day target for a well-run shop.
- The 3-pack can be crowded by competitors with hundreds of reviews. Reviews are earned over weeks and
  months; you cannot (and must not) manufacture them.
- Google states publicly that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking and tells owners to be wary of SEOs who do.
  Experienced owners have been burned by "guaranteed #1" agencies. A precise, measurable promise is
  more credible than a big vague one.

## 2. The promise to sell instead

**Headline:** "In 20 days I fix everything Google looks at, and I show you — on a map — exactly where you
rank before and after."

**What you commit to in 20 days (fully in your control):**

1. Google Business Profile fully optimised: categories, services, description, hours, attributes,
   photos, products/booking link, Q&A, first posts.
2. A fast mobile website page (or a fixed existing one) per main service, with correct local schema.
3. Name/address/phone made consistent on the main Canadian directories.
4. A review-request system the owner actually uses (QR card + text template), fully within Google's rules.
5. A before/after **rank map** (7×7 grid around the shop) for 3–5 agreed searches, plus Google Search positions.

**Target you state (measured, not guaranteed):** "Top 3 in Google Maps within [radius] km of your shop for
[2–3 main searches]." Set the radius per client from the competitor scan (B04): 1–2 km in dense Toronto,
3–5 km in Bradford/Newmarket-size towns.

**Risk-reversal (pick one, write it into the agreement exactly):**

| Option | Wording | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Movement guarantee | "If your average Maps rank across the grid hasn't improved by day 30, month 1 is free." | Default. Measurable, fair, survivable |
| B — Pay on result | "Setup fee covers the 20-day work. Monthly fee starts only once you're top 3 on at least [N] of the 49 grid points for [keyword]." | Low-competition towns where you're confident |
| C — Work guarantee only | "Every item on the 20-day checklist is delivered or that part is refunded." | Highly competitive markets (downtown Toronto) |

Never write "#1 guaranteed", "guaranteed first page" or "everyone in Toronto will see you first".

Until you have 3+ real before/after case studies, keep claims to what you've measured. (Canada's
Competition Act treats unsubstantiated performance claims as a misleading-advertising issue — see 11.
This is not legal advice; get a lawyer to review your agreement template once.)

## 3. Who to take (qualification)

Take a client only if **all** are true:

- Real, verified Google Business Profile, or one that can be verified (owner controls it, not suspended).
- Real staffed location or a legitimate service-area setup — no virtual offices.
- Owner can grant you **Manager** access to the profile (never share passwords).
- Owner will ask real customers for reviews (at least 5 customers/week for the sprint).
- Competition scan (B04) says top 3 within the target radius is plausible: e.g. the current #3 has fewer
  than ~3× the client's reviews, or a weak/incomplete profile you can out-optimise.
- Has capacity for more customers.

Say no to: suspended profiles needing reinstatement, multiple-location chains (for now), businesses that
want fake reviews or keyword-stuffed names, and "I want to rank in all of Toronto" with one shop.

## 4. Starting niches

| Niche | Why | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbers / salons (York Region, Simcoe south) | Storefront = strong proximity signal; many have weak profiles; lots of happy customers to ask for reviews | Booksy/Fresha pages compete in organic results; owner time is short |
| Pest control (GTA north) | High job value, worth paying for; service-area business | SAB rules (hide address), heavy competition in Toronto proper, lead-gen spam competitors |
| Trades (plumbing, electrical, HVAC) | High value, your engineering background helps credibility | Very competitive; long review gap to close |
| Restaurants/cafes | Lots of searches | Low margins; ranking ≠ revenue as cleanly — later |

Start with **barbers** to build fast case studies (small towns, storefronts, quick review velocity), then
use those to sell pest control and trades at higher prices.

## 5. Pricing (hypotheses — test them, all CAD, before HST)

| Package | Price | Includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot (first 2–3 clients) | $300 setup, $149/mo × 3 | Full 20-day sprint; you get a case study + testimonial permission |
| Standard — storefront (barber/salon) | $750 setup + $249/mo | Sprint + monthly: 4 posts, 2 photo batches, review tracking, 1 page improvement, rank map report |
| Standard — service-area (pest/trades) | $1,200 setup + $399/mo | Same + 2 extra service/area pages, more keywords on the grid |
| Rank map audit only (sales tool) | Free or $99 | Grid scan + 1-page "why you're not top 3" — the hook |

Monthly work is not unlimited. Normal monthly delivery target: ≤ 2 hours of your time per client. If a
client repeatedly costs more, raise the price or reduce scope.

Collect: 50% setup at signing, 50% on day 20 delivery. Monthly starts day 21 (or at result, option B).
